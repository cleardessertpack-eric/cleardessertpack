Tiramisu Container Size Guide: Portions, Dimensions & Lid Fit

By Lucy

Sizes & fitPublished November 10, 2026

Choose by usable space and lid clearance

Choose a tiramisu container by the space your finished dessert needs beneath the closed lid. Use capacity to shortlist models, then check internal fit, topping clearance and serving access with a filled sample. Outside dimensions and millilitres alone cannot confirm a fit.

This guide is for bakeries, dessert producers and packaging distributors selecting empty containers. For a complete catalogue, use the tiramisu container size chart; for order quantities, see the wholesale range.

1. Measure the dessert you actually sell

Record the finished length, width and tallest point, including cream, cocoa decoration and any base board. For a round portion, measure its widest diameter. If the dessert is assembled in the container, record the intended filling level and check how easily a spoon reaches the corners.

Keep the measurements for each menu item separate. A container that fits one recipe or topping arrangement may need another check after a product change. Record the normal portion weight during the same trial so purchasing and production are discussing the same pack.

2. Separate outside size, usable space and lid clearance

Record the measurement level and matching lid

  1. Identify the configuration. Record the body SKU and matching lid reference. Place the empty body on a level surface and distinguish catalogue outside dimensions from the usable space inside.
  2. Measure at the levels you use. Record the clear inner length and width at the base and at the intended filling level, including that level’s height above the inner base. For a round format, record the inside diameter at those levels. Tapered walls and rounded corners can reduce the space available to a dessert or base board.
  3. Check the assembled pack. Use the finished dessert and exact lid to check the tallest topping without pressing the cream. Do not assume catalogue height is usable height beneath the lid. Record the measurement points, filling level and closed-pack photos with the sample approval.

There is no single headroom allowance that suits every tiramisu. The lid profile, decoration and movement during handling matter. Start with a sample that closes without pressing the cream, then check that condition through your actual packing and carrying process.

240 ml or 350 ml: compare height as well as volume

SSM-002 and SSM-012 have the same listed 11.8 × 6 cm footprint, but different catalogue heights. Shortlist both when a rectangular portion needs more room for layers or topping. The 1.9 cm difference in listed height does not establish an extra 1.9 cm of usable clearance: check the assembled lids.

4. Choose a single-portion or sharing format

For individual sales, compare how the filled portion sits in the box, how the layers look and whether it can be packed and served cleanly. A square, round and narrow rectangular container can use display space differently even when the nominal volumes are close.

For family or catering portions, check base support and access for cutting and serving. Include any serving board in the fit check. Measure the complete closed container against the shelf and delivery carton, then evaluate the filled arrangement. A large nominal capacity does not establish a serving count or a safe stacking load.

Start a narrow rectangular comparison with SSM-002 and SSM-012: their listed footprints match, while their catalogue heights and capacities differ. For a square or round presentation, compare SSM-004 or SSM-016. For a sharing format, shortlist the larger rectangular catalogue models, then approve fit, serving access and filled handling with your actual dessert. These are catalogue shortlists, not confirmed portion weights or serving counts. Extra volume alone does not improve fit or transport performance.

5. Confirm the working fill and record the approved fit

Weigh an empty container, fill it with your actual tiramisu to the intended level, and weigh it again without the lid. Subtract the empty-container weight to record the dessert weight. Nominal capacity does not establish dessert weight: different recipes and filling levels produce different weights, including a target portion such as 250 g. Record the weight from your own filled trial.

Keep the body SKU, lid reference, dessert measurements, filling level and photos with the approval. Repeat the affected checks when the recipe, topping, lid or container changes.
