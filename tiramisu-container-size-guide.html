Tiramisu Container Size Guide: Dimensions and Lid Fit

By Lucy

Sizes & fitPublished November 10, 2026

Choose by usable dimensions and closed-lid clearance

Measure the real dessert, working fill and highest topping before choosing a catalogue volume. Compare inside space separately from outside dimensions, then check the exact body and lid with a filled sample. Keep serving access, complete-pack handling and portion weight tied to the approved recipe and format.

For bakeries, dessert producers and packaging buyers selecting empty tiramisu containers.

1. Measure the dessert before choosing a volume

Choose tiramisu container size from the usable space your finished dessert needs. Record tiramisu container dimensions at the opening, base and filling level. Bakeries sourcing tiramisu portion containers should also check topping clearance, serving access and the complete outside footprint.

Measure the finished length, width and highest point, including cream, decoration and any board. For a round portion, measure its widest diameter. If staff fill directly into the container, record the normal working line instead of measuring only an empty box.

Keep each menu item's measurements separate. A new topping or base can change the fit even when the portion weight stays the same. Take a filled-pack photo so production and purchasing can identify the same dessert.

2. Separate outside dimensions from usable space

Outside dimensions help you plan shelf space and outer packing. They do not describe the room available to the dessert. Tapered walls, rounded corners and the lid rim can reduce usable space.

Put the empty body on a level surface. Record clear inner length and width at the base and at the intended filling level. Note that level's height above the inner base. For a round format, record the inside diameter at both levels.

Fit the exact matching lid and check the smallest clearance above the tallest topping. Do not press the cream down to make the sample close. Catalogue height is not the same as usable height beneath the closed lid.

Keep the measurement points, tool, body reference and lid reference with the result. A measurement-point schematic can show where to measure; it does not supply model dimensions or a universal clearance value.

4. Compare height as well as capacity

SSM-002 and SSM-012 share a listed 11.8 × 6 cm footprint. Their listed heights are 5.1 cm and 7 cm, and their nominal capacities differ. Shortlist both when a narrow portion needs more space for layers or topping.

The 1.9 cm difference in catalogue height does not establish an extra 1.9 cm of usable lid clearance. Compare the assembled samples and the real dessert. A lid profile or internal base can change the working space.

For square or round presentation, compare SSM-004 and SSM-016 with the actual portion shape. Similar nominal volumes do not mean identical display use, corner access or serving space.

5. Choose the single-portion or sharing workflow

For individual sales, check how the filled portion sits and whether staff can pack it cleanly. Try a spoon at the corners and check how the customer can serve the dessert. Review how much of the layers remain visible after closing and branding.

For a sharing dessert, check base support, cutting access and how staff lift the filled container. Include a serving board if the real process uses one. Compare the complete closed pack with the shelf, carrier and delivery carton.

Large capacity does not establish a safe stack height or a fixed number of servings. Use the actual filled mass and handling arrangement for those checks. Extra volume alone does not improve fit or transport performance.

6. Record a working weight from the real recipe

Weigh an empty container. Fill it to the working line with your tiramisu, then weigh it again without the lid. Subtract the empty-container mass to record the dessert weight for that recipe and filling level.

Use this trial to review a target portion such as 250 g. Do not assume a 250 ml container holds 250 g of every dessert. Recipe, air, layers and filling level affect the result.

Record the actual filled mass again when you assess stacking or carrying. Add the lid and any board or outer protection for the complete-pack handling trial.

7. Retain the approved fit for purchasing

Keep the body model, matching lid, dessert measurements, filling line and photos together. Record who approved the fit and which sample the supplier should follow. Review the affected checks when the recipe, topping, lid or body changes.
