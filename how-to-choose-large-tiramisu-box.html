PS vs PET Tiramisu Containers: Wholesale Buyer Guide

By Lucy

PS vs PETPublished July 21, 2026Updated November 11, 2026

Compare the finished format and its evidence

Compare PS and PET containers around the actual dessert, usable space, matching lid and handling route. Keep body and lid materials separate, verify the selected use conditions and review complete quotations. Catalogue capacity and a resin name are selection references, not approval for every storage or delivery process.

For bakeries, dessert producers and packaging buyers selecting empty tiramisu containers.

1. Compare the finished containers

Compare PS vs PET tiramisu containers using the same dessert and handling conditions. Material is one part of the choice. Shape, wall design, lid fit and the way staff support the filled pack also affect the result.

For plastic tiramisu containers, begin with the use you need to approve. Will the pack stay in a display, leave at the counter or travel in a carrier? Shortlist PS and PET dessert containers that can meet that brief, then test the exact body and matching lid.

Do not treat two different capacities as a controlled material comparison. A large sharing tray and a smaller box can differ in shape, load and support needs as well as resin.

2. Confirm the material for each component

Check the body material shown for the selected model. Confirm the lid material separately. A body marked PS does not automatically establish the lid material, and a clear wall does not identify the resin by sight alone.

Customers sometimes use “acrylic tiramisu container” to describe a clear PS box. In the listed PS range, the specified body material is polystyrene. Do not turn that informal description into a claim that the box is PMMA acrylic.

Acrylic cocoa stencils are a separate accessory. Their material does not describe the dessert container. Send a model or reference photo when the product name is unclear so the supplier can confirm the intended item.

3. Shortlist catalogue formats with their dimensions

HK-28188 lists a 4000 ml PS body with catalogue dimensions of 28 × 18 × 8 cm.

HK-21157 lists an 1800 ml PS body with catalogue dimensions of 21 × 15 × 7 cm.

E1300 lists a 1300 ml PET body with catalogue dimensions of 18 × 13 × 6.4 cm.

These are catalogue references for selection. They are not internal dimensions, portion weights or approved serving counts. Compare the size as well as the material before you ask for samples.

For smaller portions, the catalogue also lists SSM-002 at 240 ml and SSM-004 at 450 ml. The size chart provides the model references. Check the usable space and matching lid for each candidate rather than assuming capacity alone proves the fit.

4. Measure usable space with the actual dessert

Measure the dessert's length, width and highest topping, including any board. If staff fill directly into the box, record the normal working line. Check the inner base and the opening at the level you use.

Fit the exact lid and look for cream contact, raised corners and gaps. Catalogue outside height is not the usable height beneath a closed lid. Confirm the minimum clearance on an assembled sample.

For a narrow format, SSM-002 lists 11.8 × 6 × 5.1 cm, while SSM-012 lists 11.8 × 6 × 7 cm. The footprint matches, but the assembled working space still needs a sample check. A difference in catalogue height does not establish the same difference in usable lid clearance.

Use the tiramisu container size guide for a measurement record. Keep each recipe, fill level and body-and-lid reference separate so the buyer can identify what the kitchen approved.

5. Test the real storage, display and carrying process

Fill the samples with your normal dessert. Weigh the complete pack and inspect the base, rim and corners. Compare closure and presentation through the process the order will use.

For cold storage, request the selected model's documented use conditions. A PET label or a refrigerated product description does not approve every freezing process. Check the body and lid together for the intended conditions.

Observe visibility and condensation during actual display stages. Clear plastic does not prove an anti-fog treatment. Any specific anti-fog requirement needs a defined product scope and a sample trial.

For delivery, use the intended carrier, tray and outer protection. Review the pack after the route for loose lids, cracks, dessert movement and topping damage. Check filled stacking separately when the process uses more than one layer.

Follow the filled-sample checklist to record the result. Packaging selection does not set food-storage times or extend shelf life; keep the trial within your validated food-handling process.

6. Review evidence for the chosen pack

Request documents that identify the material or item and intended use. Record the supplier, reference and scope. A general resin description is not a product-specific food-contact approval for every model and destination.

If a project requires recycled content, ask for the percentage, component and supporting record. Standard PET wording does not establish rPET content. Do not transfer a claim from a different supplier or material version.

The FDA recycled-plastics guidance concerns the evaluation of recycling processes for food-contact applications. It is a review reference, not a certification of these containers. Keep environmental claims in a separate evidence review for the exact pack.

7. Compare complete quotations on the same basis

Give each supplier the selected body and lid references, quantities and counting units. State whether a sleeve, label or outer protection belongs in the set. Ask the quote to show which components the unit price includes.

Confirm current carton counts, outside carton dimensions and gross mass before comparing freight. A body count and lid count cannot simply be added together to give a complete-set quantity.

The standard plastic box offer starts at one carton. Confirm custom accessories, mixed-model quantities, sample charges and timing separately for the chosen order. A standard-box offer does not approve an unreviewed custom structure.

Use the wholesale buying guide to keep the sample, specification and quotation aligned. Compare total cost within the same agreed scope rather than ranking PS and PET from unmatched unit prices.

8. Keep the approved version for repeat orders

Retain the body model, matching lid, material record, fill line and sample photos. Include storage, display and delivery results with the approval. Identify who approves the physical pack and who places the order.

Review the affected checks if the material, lid, geometry, recipe or packing changes. Choose the format that meets the actual task and evidence requirements. Neither a material name nor a capacity figure can replace that complete-pack decision.
