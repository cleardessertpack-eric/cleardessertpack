Custom branding

Paper sleeves or logo stickers? Find the right fit.

Explore two ways to brand a clear dessert box, from artwork space to fit and assembly.

In the highly visual world of Instagram, TikTok, and luxury retail, dessert packaging is the first point of tactile communication. A plain, unbranded transparent plastic box risks looking like generic supermarket commodity stock. Custom branding bridges that gap, telling customers that the premium product inside is worth every dollar of its premium price.

When designing custom packaging kits, the two most common entry points for branding are custom paper sleeves (belly bands) and high-grade logo stickers. While simple on paper, picking the wrong format can clog your kitchen's packaging speed, inflate your unit cost, or leave you with loose, slipping sleeves that slide right off your boxes.

Use the comparison below to prepare a quotation and sample review. Costs, application speed and fit depend on the selected design, material, quantity and packing workflow.

1. Sleeves vs. stickers

Compare sleeves and stickers against the same box, artwork requirements and order quantity. Neither option has a universal cost or volume advantage:

Upfront Setup Cost Confirm artwork, cutting, printing and any tooling charges for the selected design. Confirm artwork, cutting, printing and finishing charges for the selected design.
Unit price and MOQ Request a quote by paper, dimensions, print finish and quantity; confirm the MOQ separately. Request a quote by label dimensions, substrate, adhesive, finish and quantity; confirm the MOQ separately.

Key B2B Takeaway: Compare the total cost of the approved accessory, application labour, packing losses and freight. Measure assembly time with your staff and samples before calculating savings; neither format has a guaranteed time or cost advantage.

2. Getting the fit right

Sleeve retention depends on the assembled box profile, sleeve material, fold positions and handling conditions. Check the chosen sample for slipping, interference with the lid and excessive pressure on the box.

Include these three points in the sample review:

  • Retention method: Ask whether a closure or adhesive is needed. Confirm its placement, suitability and removal behaviour for the selected package.
  • Paper and finish: Compare samples for appearance, retention and performance in the intended storage and handling conditions.
  • Crease positions: Confirm the dieline, material thickness and fold positions using the assembled box and lid. Agree tolerances for the actual design.

3. Measuring a sleeve

When ordering flat paper sleeves from a factory, never guess the dimensions. A paper sleeve must fit the assembled box and lid without obstructing closure or distorting the package.

For an initial estimate around a rectangular box cross-section, use the expression below. It simplifies the shape and is not a production dieline; taper, lid projections, corners, folds, paper thickness and closure design require sample verification:

Lsleeve = 2 × (Widthbox + Heightbox) + Overlap + Clearance

Where:

  • Widthbox: The exact outer width of the box at its widest lip.
  • Heightbox: The exact outer height of the box, including the lid.
  • Overlap: The join area for the selected closure design. Confirm its length and construction with the supplier; there is no single value established here for all orders.
  • Clearance (Crease Allowance): An allowance to be determined for the actual material, folds and fit. Confirm it through the dieline and assembled sample rather than applying a fixed allowance to every box.

Hypothetical calculation — not a product specification

For illustration only, assume 15.5cm width, 6.5cm height, an overlap of 2.5cm and an allowance of 0.2cm. These inputs are hypothetical and are not an approved Clear Dessert Pack model or production allowance:

Lsleeve = 2 × (15.5 + 6.5) + 2.5 + 0.2 = 46.7 cm

The arithmetic gives an initial estimate of 46.7cm for these assumed inputs. Do not order from this example. Confirm the final sleeve length, band width, fold positions and closure using the approved dieline and an assembled sample.

4. Choosing your approach

Choose according to the information to display, assembly process and quotation for your actual quantities:

When a compact label suits the brief

Option: Existing Box + Logo Sticker

Consider a label when the required information fits the chosen surface and you want to keep much of the dessert visible. Confirm label placement, adhesive compatibility, quantity per design and application method with a sample.

When several printed panels suit the brief

Option: Existing Box + Printed Paper Sleeve

Consider a sleeve when the design needs several panels for branding and product information. Confirm legibility, folds, seam placement, lid access and assembly time. Request a quotation for the actual material and quantity before choosing.

5. Prepare a tiramisu branding quotation brief

Use the same brief when comparing a sleeve and a sticker. Choose the box model and matching lid first, then review the available branding services and scope.

  • Container: model, matching lid, assembled sample or drawing, finished dessert dimensions and intended handling conditions.
  • Accessory: sleeve, label, bag or ribbon; placement, dimensions, material and finish requirements; quantity for each design.
  • Artwork: logo file, text, colours, barcode or QR code, and the approved dieline revision. AI, PDF or SVG is preferred; other files need review.
  • Order: initial quantity, expected repeat quantities, destination country and postcode, packing preferences and requested delivery date.
  • Approval: confirm proof and sample requirements, any artwork or tooling charges, MOQ, production timing and the final approved version in writing.

Ask for the container, lid, branding accessories, setup costs and delivery scope to be identified separately. A design concept is not a completed customer project or evidence of an approved production sample.
